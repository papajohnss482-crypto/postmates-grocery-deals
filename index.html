Postmates Grocery Deals, Tracked Honestly

Grocery deals on Postmates do not work like restaurant deals. There is no reliable public grocery code. The savings live in store offer tags inside the app, and they change by store, by city, and by week. This tracker records what was actually seen in the app, with the date and city on every observation, so you can learn the pattern instead of chasing expired screenshots.

The pattern in one line: check the store's own offer tags first, let Uber One carry the delivery fee, and treat any public grocery code as a bonus that will probably be store specific. Everything below is the evidence for that, dated.

How grocery deals on Postmates actually work

  • Store offer tags are the main event. A store carries its own tags, a dollar amount off, a percentage, or a count of offers on the listing. They are set per store and per city, and they rotate.
  • Promo codes mostly do apply to grocery carts, the same as restaurant carts, unless the code's terms exclude the merchant or the category. When a code fails on groceries, the cause is usually an excluded item in the cart.
  • Excluded items are the trap. Alcohol, tobacco, and pharmacy items are often excluded from promotions. One excluded item can block a code for the whole order.
  • In-store pricing is a quiet saving. Some stores, CVS in our Dallas observation, price delivery items at shelf prices. No tag, no code, but no delivery markup either.
  • Targeted promotions sit in your account. Grocery promotions aimed at your account appear under Account, Promotions, not on any public list. Check there before every order, it takes ten seconds.

Grocery versus restaurant saving, honestly compared

Restaurant saving on Postmates has structure: set programs like Meal Deals with published terms. Grocery saving is opportunistic: whatever tags your stores carry this week, plus fee savings from membership. That is why grocery deal content online is so often stale, it describes one store, in one city, on one day, as if it were a national offer. This tracker refuses to do that. Every claim here names its store, its city, and its date, or it does not appear.
