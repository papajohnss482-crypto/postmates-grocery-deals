Find Grocery Deals in the App: The 2-Minute Routine

Grocery deals on Postmates are not published anywhere as a list. They are distributed across store listings and your own account. This routine checks all of the places they hide, in the order a regular user should check them.

The routine

  1. Start on the store's listing, not on a code site. Open the grocery or convenience store you plan to order from. Look for the offer tags on the listing, a dollar amount off, or a count of offers available, the way 7-Eleven showed a "$5 off" tag with 3 offers available in Dallas on September 26, 2026.
  2. Open the store's offers. If the listing shows offers available, open them and read the minimums before you build the cart. A great tag with a minimum above your basket is worth nothing.
  3. Check Account, Promotions. Grocery promotions targeted at your account live here. This is also where any code you add gets confirmed, with the real terms and expiry for your account.
  4. Check the Offers tab. Rotating store offers surface here between your regular stores. It is the fastest way to notice a store you use running a tag this week.
  5. Build the cart, then audit it for excluded items. Alcohol, tobacco, and pharmacy items are often excluded from promotions. If a code fails, remove those items temporarily and retry. Most grocery code failures are one excluded item, not an expired code.
  6. Compare the fee line before you pay. If you have Uber One, confirm the delivery fee shows $0 and your subtotal is over the program minimum. If a store shows in-store prices, like CVS did in our Dallas check, weigh that against a tagged store's markup plus discount.

The three mistakes that cost grocery shoppers the most

1. Trusting an undated grocery code list

Grocery offers are per store and per city. A code list with no city and no date is describing someone else's app, in some other week. If a list cannot tell you where and when a deal was seen, discount it completely. This principle is why the tracker log dates and places every entry.

2. Building the cart before reading the tag terms

Minimums and category exclusions are printed on the offer itself. Two minutes reading them beats twenty minutes wondering why the total never moved.

3. Ignoring in-store pricing

A store with no discount tag but shelf prices can beat a store with a $5 tag and marked-up items. Compare the same basket's total, not the size of the tag.

What to do when nothing is on offer

Some weeks your stores simply carry no tags. The fallback order is: use the fee saving if you have membership, use any targeted promotion waiting in your Promotions section, or shift the shop to a store showing in-store prices. What not to do is burn time trying public codes one by one. Public codes are mostly restaurant and new user instruments, and the grocery section of the method page explains what this tracker found about that.
